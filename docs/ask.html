Data AI

Ask anything, by typing or by voice. Figures come from the open datasets on this site, each with its year and its source. Explanations around them are written by an AI model and are marked as such.

Try

What you can ask

One number

A measure and a place: Kenya internet users, Japan population 1990. You get the value, how it ranks, how it moved in ten years and the full history.

A comparison

A measure and several places: Germany vs France vs Spain renewable electricity. Values side by side and one chart with a line for each.

A ranking

Words like top, highest or lowest: lowest child mortality, top 5 wind power. You get the list, and each name leads to that country's own answer.

Why and how

Why did oil prices jump in 2022? Is Africa getting more electricity? The AI reads the figures first, then explains them in plain words.

Anything else

Follow-up questions, definitions, or a topic outside the data. You still get an answer, with a clear label when it comes from general knowledge.

How it answers

  1. Plain lookups are instant. A question such as India life expectancy is matched to a variable and read straight from the data. No AI is involved, so the number cannot be invented.
  2. Everything else goes to an AI model. When it needs a figure it asks this page for it, and the page reads the value from the same datasets. The cards under an AI answer show exactly what was read.
  3. General knowledge is labelled. If the datasets hold nothing on a topic, the AI answers from what it knows and says so. Like any AI it can be wrong or out of date there, so check anything that matters.

Questions for the AI are sent to a Claude model through this site's small service and are not stored with your name. Voice input uses your browser's speech recognition, which in some browsers sends the audio to the browser's maker. Please do not include personal details. The AI has a daily allowance; when it is used up, answers come straight from the datasets until the next day.
